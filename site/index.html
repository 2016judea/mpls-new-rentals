952 Minneapolis rentals with a new license, and what the record says about their heat

Minneapolis requires a safety check of any rental heating plant over ten years old every two years, and a newly licensed rental gets its first city inspection. These are the rentals that changed owners or became rentals since April 1, or let their March 1 license renewal lapse. At 649 of them the city has no heating permit on file since 2016, so the furnace or boiler is likely past ten years.

    Sources, all public: the City of Minneapolis rental license register and building permit register, pulled Oct 17, 2026, and Hennepin County parcel records for year built. The ten-year rule is from the city's Rental Property Checklist (July 2025); the city's HVAC safety check form is valid for two years.

    How heat is read. A heating permit is a Minneapolis mechanical permit at the same parcel whose description names a furnace, boiler, heat pump or heating work. The register starts in December 2016, so "no heating permit since 2016" means none on record, not proof of the age of the plant. In a building with several units, one permit may cover one unit.

    Owner contact is shown for companies and for landlords with five or more licensed units, as the city publishes it. Owners of one to four unit rentals are left unnamed. Minneapolis only. MPHA public housing is left out.
